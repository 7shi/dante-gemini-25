1. Having lost the straight path in the dark wood, the narrator tries to climb a hill but is driven back by three beasts, especially the she-wolf, until Virgil appears, promises to guide him through Hell and Purgatory, and receives his consent.
2. Dante, hesitant and convinced he is unworthy of the journey through the afterlife, is encouraged by Virgil, who reveals the intervention of Beatrice and three blessed ladies, and resumes the path with him as his guide and master.
3. Dante and Virgil enter Hell, where they see the tormented neutrals, meet Charon ferrying the damned souls across Acheron, and Dante faints as the earth trembles.
4. Dante and Virgil descend into Limbo, where virtuous unbaptized souls live without hope; welcomed by the great poets, Dante visits the castle of illustrious spirits before resuming his journey with his guide.
5. In the second circle, Minos judges the lustful swept by the eternal storm; Dante meets Francesca and Paolo, hears the story of their love born while reading Lancelot, and, overcome by pity, faints.
6. In the third circle, Dante and Virgil calm Cerberus, meet the glutton Ciacco, who prophesies Florence’s conflicts and identifies other damned souls, and then learn that punishments will increase after Judgment, finally reaching Plutus.
7. Virgil subdues Plutus and leads Dante into the fourth circle, where the avaricious and prodigal push weights and clash, explains Fortune to him, then the two reach the Styx marsh, where the wrathful and sullen suffer their punishment.
8. Dante and Virgil cross the Styx marsh with Phlegyas, repel Filippo Argenti, reach the city of Dis, and are stopped by demons at its gates, until Virgil announces a rescuer who will open the way for them.
9. At Dis's gates, Virgil protects Dante from the Furies and Medusa until a heavenly messenger opens the city; the two enter and see heretics tormented in fiery tombs.
10. Among the tombs of the heretics, Dante meets Farinata and Cavalcante, learns the damned's limited foreknowledge, and receives from Farinata the prophecy of his own exile before resuming the journey with Virgil.
11. Dante and Virgil, pausing beside Pope Anastasius’s tomb to grow accustomed to the abyss’s stench, discuss the ordering of the lower circles and the sins of violence, fraud, incontinence, and usury, then resume their journey toward the seventh circle.
12. Dante and Virgil pass the Minotaur and descend into the circle of the violent, where, guided by the centaur Nessus, they cross the boiling-blood Phlegethon, observing tyrants and other violent sinners punished according to the gravity of their sins.
13. In the second round, Dante and Virgil cross the wood of the suicides, where Pier della Vigna recounts his downfall and the punishment of souls turned into trees, while Jacopo da Santo Andrea is torn apart by infernal hounds.
14. Dante and Virgil enter the third round of the seventh circle, where a rain of fire torments the violent against God, encounter the proud Capaneus, and follow Phlegethon as Virgil explains its origin in the Old Man of Crete.
15. Guided by Virgil along the embankments of the Phlegethon, Dante meets his teacher Brunetto Latini, who predicts glory and Florentine hostility for him, names other damned souls in his troop, and then takes leave running back to it.
16. Dante and Virgil meet three noble Florentines among the sodomites, to whom Dante deplores Florence’s corruption, then reach the waterfall where Virgil throws Dante’s cord and Geryon appears.
17. Virgil presents Geryon, figure of fraud, sends Dante to see the tormented usurers, and then, both mounted on the monster, the two poets descend terrified through the air to the eighth circle.
18. Dante and Virgil cross the first two ditches of Malebolge, where demons whip seducers and panders, including Venedico Caccianemico and Jason, while flatterers, including Alessio Interminei and Thaïs, are immersed in excrement.
19. In the third ditch of the eighth circle, Dante and Virgil encounter simoniacs fixed in fiery holes; Nicholas III mistakes Dante for Boniface VIII and predicts Clement V, while Dante condemns papal corruption before Virgil leads him to the next ditch.
20. In the ditch of the diviners, damned to walk with their faces turned backward, Virgil rebukes Dante’s pity, shows him famous seers, and recounts Mantua’s true foundation by Manto before the two resume their journey.
21. Dante and Virgil reach the ditch of the barrators immersed in boiling pitch, where Virgil humbles Malacoda by declaring Heaven’s will, and the two, escorted by the Malebranche despite Dante’s fear, set out toward the next embankment.
22. Led by the Malebranche along the bolgia of the barrators, Dante and Virgil witness the questioning of a Navarrese, who deceives the demons by fleeing into the pitch and causes a fight between Alichino and Calcabrina, left stuck in the boiling pool.
23. Dante and Virgil escape the Malebranche into the sixth ditch, where they meet hypocrites in leaden cloaks, learn from friars Catalano and Loderingo of Caiaphas's punishment and the route through the ruins, and Virgil discovers the devil's deceit.
24. Reassured by Virgil and led beyond the ruined bridge, Dante reaches the eighth ditch, where he sees thieves tormented by serpents and witnesses Vanni Fucci's rebirth; Fucci confesses the sacrilegious theft and predicts the Whites' defeat.
25. In the seventh ditch, Vanni Fucci prophesies the defeat of the Whites and is punished by snakes for his blasphemy, while Dante sees Cacus and witnesses the thieves’ terrifying reciprocal metamorphoses, recognizing Puccio Sciancato and the man for whom Gaville weeps.
26. Dante condemns Florence, contemplates in the eighth ditch the flames enclosing the fraudulent counselors, and hears Ulysses recount his voyage beyond the Pillars of Hercules, ending in shipwreck near an unknown mountain.
27. As Ulysses departs, Dante questions Guido da Montefeltro, who recounts how Boniface VIII induced him to give fraudulent counsel with a false absolution, condemning him among the fraudulent counselors of the eighth ditch.
28. In the ninth ditch Dante sees the sowers of scandal and schism, endlessly mutilated by a demon’s sword, hears Muhammad and Pier da Medicina with their prophecies, and meets Mosca and Bertram dal Bornio, punished for the discord they sowed.
29. Virgil draws Dante away from his pity for Geri del Bello, then the two reach the final ditch, where among the sick falsifiers they meet Griffolino of Arezzo and Capocchio, who sarcastically denounce the vanity of the Sienese.
30. Dante observes the falsifiers, including Gianni Schicchi and Myrrha, then Master Adam, tormented by dropsy and thirst, quarrels with Sinon until Virgil rebukes the poet for taking pleasure in their vulgar dispute.
31. Virgil leads Dante from the valley of the eighth ditch to the pit of the giants, where the chained Nimrod and Ephialtes inspire terror, and persuades free Antaeus to set them down near Cocytus, at Hell’s bottom.
32. Dante and Virgil descend into frozen Cocytus, where Dante encounters and denounces traitors to kin and homeland, including Bocca degli Abati, before seeing a damned soul ferociously devour his enemy’s head.
33. Count Ugolino tells Dante of the hunger that killed his sons in the tower through Archbishop Ruggieri’s fault; then Dante crosses Ptolemea, exposes Friar Alberigo, and condemns Pisa and Genoa.
34. Virgil leads Dante before Lucifer, the monstrous ruler of Cocytus who crushes Judas, Brutus, and Cassius, then the two pass the center of the Earth by climbing his body and ascend until they see the stars again.
