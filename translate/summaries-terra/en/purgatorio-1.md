1. Having emerged from Hell, Dante beholds Purgatory's sky and Cato, who, persuaded by Virgil sent by a heavenly woman, lets them pass and orders the poet purified and girded with a reed.
2. At dawn an angel brings souls, including Casella, to Purgatory; he sings for Dante until Cato rebukes everyone and urges them to run toward the mountain.
3. Dante and Virgil, having clarified the mystery of the shadow and the limits of human reason, meet at the mountain’s foot the souls of the excommunicated, including Manfred, who recounts his repentance and asks his daughter Constance for prayers.
4. Dante and Virgil laboriously climb Purgatory’s steep mountain, rest on a ledge where Virgil explains the sun’s position, then meet Belacqua, who awaits permission to begin penance, and resume their journey.
5. The souls of those who died violently recognize Dante as alive and ask to be remembered on earth: Jacopo del Cassero recounts his murder, Bonconte da Montefeltro his soul’s salvation and his body’s disappearance, and Pia allusively reveals her death.
6. Dante and Virgil meet the souls of those who died violent deaths, clarify the value of prayers, and, guided by Sordello after his embrace with Virgil, prompt Dante’s invectives against divided Italy and unstable Florence.
7. Sordello recognizes and honors Virgil, guides him and Dante into the valley of princes, where amid the singing of the “Salve, Regina” he points out various negligent or degenerate rulers, declaring that virtue rarely passes through lineage.
8. At sunset the souls pray as two angels drive the serpent from the valley, and Dante meets Nino Visconti and Currado Malaspina, who entrusts him with a message for his daughter and predicts the fulfillment of his praise for the Malaspinas.
9. At dawn Dante dreams of an eagle that abducts him, then learns that Lucia has carried him to Purgatory’s gate, where the angel inscribes seven P’s on his forehead and, after his humble prayer, opens the entrance to the singing of the “Te Deum.”
10. Dante and Virgil reach the first terrace, where Dante contemplates divine examples of humility carved in marble and sees the souls of the proud, bent beneath enormous stones to atone for their sin.
11. On the first terrace, the souls of the proud pray beneath boulders; Dante meets Omberto Aldobrandesco and Oderisi da Gubbio, who condemns the vanity of fame, and learns how Provenzan Salvani humbled himself to save a friend.
12. Virgil turns Dante away from the bent soul and shows him on the pavement examples of punished proud figures; then an angel erases one P from his forehead, and the two climb more lightly toward the next terrace.
13. Dante and Virgil reach the terrace of the envious, hear examples of charity, and meet souls with sewn eyes, including Sapia, who confesses her envy and asks the living Dante to renew her memory in Tuscany.
14. Dante meets Guido del Duca and Rinieri da Calboli among the envious, hears the condemnation of the corruption of the Arno and Romagna, and, departing with Virgil, hears terrible examples of punished envy and the explanation of their warning.
15. Dante and Virgil receive from the angel of mercy the invitation to ascend, discuss shared good, then Dante beholds examples of meekness in a vision before entering the smoke of the terrace of wrath.
16. In the smoke of the wrathful Dante meets Marco Lombardo, who attributes the world's corruption to human free will and the confusion of the Church's powers, then names the few virtuous people remaining before taking leave at dawn.
17. Having emerged from the smoke, Dante is carried away in visions of examples of punished wrath, receives from the angel access to the fourth terrace, and, stopping with Virgil at nightfall, hears love explained as the root of every virtue and sin.
18. Virgil explains to Dante that love, though naturally moved by apparent good, makes man morally responsible through free will; then, among souls running to expiate sloth and crying out examples of it, Dante falls asleep and dreams.
19. Dante dreams of the seductive Siren, unmasked by Virgil, then climbs to the fifth terrace, where Pope Adrian V explains the punishment of the avaricious and entrusts him with remembrance of his niece Alagia.
20. Dante and Virgil leave Adrian V, hear Hugh Capet condemn avarice and the crimes of his descendants, then witness the mountain’s trembling and the souls’ song, resuming their journey full of wonder.
21. As Dante and Virgil ask the reason for Purgatory's earthquake, the freed soul of Statius explains the sign of purification, celebrates Virgil as his poetic master, and lovingly discovers that he stands before him.
22. Having climbed to the sixth terrace after the angel erases another P, Dante, Virgil, and Statius discuss Statius’s prodigality and hidden conversion, recall the poets of Limbo, and reach the tree that teaches temperance.
23. Dante meets the glutton Forese Donati, who explains the purifying punishment and credits his swift ascent to Nella’s prayers; Dante then recounts his journey with Virgil toward Beatrice and points out the liberated Statius.
24. Dante speaks with Forese and Bonagiunta, who recognizes the sweet new style and prophesies Gentucca; he receives from Forese a prophecy of Florence’s ruin, then with Virgil and Statius passes the gluttons’ second tree and ascends toward the next terrace.
25. As they ascend toward the terrace of the lustful, Statius explains to Dante the generation of the soul and of shades, then the pilgrims reach the purifying flame, crossed by souls singing examples of chastity.
26. In the circle of the lustful, Dante, astonishing the souls because still alive, learns the distinction between the two bands, devoutly meets Guido Guinizzelli and Arnaut Daniel, who ask him for remembrance and prayers before vanishing into the flames.
27. Dante crosses with Virgil and Statius the flame that separates him from Beatrice, dreams of Leah and Rachel, and, having reached the Earthly Paradise, receives from Virgil recognition of the full freedom of his own will.
28. Dante crosses the forest of Eden and meets Matelda beside a clear stream, who explains the nature of the place, its breezes and waters, Lethe and Eunoë, and the memory of lost innocence.
29. Guided by Matelda, Dante witnesses in the forest of the Earthly Paradise the luminous allegorical procession of the seven candelabra, the twenty-four elders, the animals, and the triumphal chariot drawn by the griffin, which stops before him.
30. Amid angelic songs and a rain of flowers, veiled Beatrice appears, Virgil disappears, and, after reproaching Dante for losing himself in false goods, she demands his tears of repentance before Lethe.
31. Beatrice compels Dante to confess his straying after her death, reproves him until he faints with shame, and Matelda purifies him in Lethe before the virtues lead him to contemplate Beatrice’s shining eyes.
32. Dante follows Beatrice and the procession to the renewed tree, receives from her the order to watch the chariot, and witnesses its transformation, after allegorical assaults, into a beast dragged into the forest by the giant with the prostitute.
33. Beatrice announces her return, prophesies the punishment of the Church’s corruption, and charges Dante to report the vision; then Matelda leads him to Eunoë, whose waters renew him and prepare him to rise to the stars.
