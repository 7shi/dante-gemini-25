1. Lost in a dark wood and blocked from a sunlit hill by three beasts, the narrator is rescued by the poet Virgil, who offers to guide him on a journey through Hell.
2. Virgil quells the narrator's doubt by explaining how Beatrice descended from Heaven to commission their journey, which renews the narrator's resolve to proceed.
3. After passing through Hell's gate and observing the neutral souls, Dante confronts the ferryman Charon at the river Acheron and faints from terror as he is about to cross.
4. Dante and Virgil journey through Limbo, where they meet the great classical poets and see the noble but hopeless souls of virtuous pagans dwelling in a magnificent castle without hope of salvation.
5. In the Second Circle, Dante meets souls punished for lust and, after hearing the tragic story of Francesca and Paolo, faints from overwhelming pity.
6. In the Third Circle of Gluttony, Virgil pacifies the monstrous Cerberus, allowing Dante to speak with the Florentine shade Ciacco, who delivers a dark prophecy concerning their city's political future.
7. After silencing Plutus, Virgil guides Dante through the circle of the avaricious and prodigal, explains the nature of Fortune, and descends to the Stygian marsh to find the wrathful and sullen.
8. After crossing the Styx and angrily confronting Filippo Argenti, Dante and Virgil are denied entry into the city of Dis by rebellious fallen angels who slam the gates in their faces.
9. After being threatened by the Furies at the gates of Dis, Virgil and the narrator gain entry with the help of a heavenly messenger, revealing the fiery tombs of the heretics within the Sixth Circle.
10. Among the fiery tombs of the heretics, Dante speaks with Farinata about Florentine politics and receives a prophecy of his own exile, while also encountering Cavalcante, who despairs over his son's fate.
11. While pausing before the Seventh Circle, Virgil explains the moral structure of Lower Hell to Dante, distinguishing the graver sins of malice and fraud from the lesser sins of incontinence.
12. After bypassing the enraged Minotaur, Virgil and the narrator are guided by the centaur Nessus along a river of boiling blood where souls of the violent are submerged to varying depths.
13. In a dark forest, Dante discovers suicides transformed into bleeding trees, speaks with Pier della Vigna, and then witnesses wastrels being hunted and torn apart by vicious hounds.
14. Upon reaching a fiery plain, the poets meet the proud blasphemer Capaneus and learn from Virgil that Hell's rivers flow from the tears of the Old Man of Crete.
15. Walking across the burning plain, Dante has a respectful encounter with his former mentor, Brunetto Latini, who prophesies the poet's future suffering at the hands of the Florentines.
16. Dante converses with three noble Florentine souls about their city's decline before Virgil uses Dante's cord to summon a monstrous figure from the abyss below the waterfall.
17. After observing the tormented usurers, Dante and Virgil mount the monstrous Geryon, the embodiment of fraud, who flies them down the great abyss into the eighth circle of Hell.
18. In the Eighth Circle's first two ditches, Dante and Virgil witness panderers and seducers being whipped by demons and flatterers submerged in human excrement.
19. Dante confronts the simonist Pope Nicholas III in a fiery ditch, condemns papal avarice, and is then carried to the next chasm by a pleased Virgil.
20. After rebuking Dante's pity for the diviners whose heads are twisted backward, Virgil identifies famous seers and explains how the prophetess Manto gave her name to his native city, Mantua.
21. In the ditch of boiling pitch, Virgil confronts the menacing Malebranche demons, who deceive the poets and assign them a demonic escort to a supposedly intact bridge.
22. The sinner Ciampolo cleverly tricks his demon captors and escapes into the boiling pitch, sparking a brawl that allows the narrator and Virgil to slip away during the chaos.
23. After escaping the Malebranche, Virgil and Dante meet the hypocrites wearing lead-lined cloaks, see the crucified Caiaphas, and learn from a friar that the demon Malacoda had lied to them about the path forward.
24. After a difficult climb, Dante and Virgil enter the seventh ditch of thieves, where the sinner Vanni Fucci, tormented by snakes, is restored from ash and delivers a spiteful prophecy to the narrator.
25. After Vanni Fucci's blasphemy, Dante observes the horrifying punishment of the thieves, who undergo grotesque metamorphoses by merging with and exchanging forms with serpents.
26. In the ditch of fraudulent counselors, Dante and Virgil listen as the flame-enshrouded Ulysses recounts his final, fatal voyage beyond the known world in pursuit of knowledge.
27. The soul of Guido da Montefeltro recounts how Pope Boniface VIII coerced him into giving fraudulent counsel with a false promise of absolution, leading directly to his damnation.
28. In the ninth ditch, Dante witnesses the horrific, continuous mutilation of the sowers of discord, including Muhammad and Bertrand de Born, who explains his punishment as a fitting retribution for his divisive actions.
29. Dante enters the final ditch of Malebolge, where he speaks with two alchemists, Griffolino and Capocchio, who are punished for their falsifications with a tormenting, scabby disease.
30. Dante witnesses the punishments of various falsifiers, culminating in a vulgar quarrel between the counterfeiter Master Adam and the liar Sinon, for which Virgil rebukes him for his unseemly interest.
31. After encountering the giants guarding the central pit, Dante and Virgil are persuaded by the giant Antaeus to lower them into the Ninth and final Circle of Hell.
32. Descending to the frozen lake of Cocytus, Dante confronts traitors to their kin and country, including Bocca degli Abati, before discovering one soul horrifically gnawing on the head of another.
33. After hearing Count Ugolino's gruesome tale of starvation, Dante meets Friar Alberigo, who explains that the souls of traitors to guests are damned to hell while their bodies live on Earth possessed by demons.
34. After witnessing the monstrous, three-faced Lucifer, Virgil carries Dante down the giant's body, passing the earth's center to climb out of Hell and emerge at last to see the stars.
