1. Lost in a dark wood and blocked by three beasts, Dante meets the shade of Virgil, who rescues him and offers to guide him on a journey through Hell and Purgatory to save him.
2. Dante, seized by doubt about his own adequacy, is reassured by Virgil, who reveals he was sent by Beatrice—in turn moved by Lucia and the Virgin Mary—thus restoring his courage to begin the journey.
3. Having passed the Gate of Hell, Dante sees the punishment of the slothful in the Ante-Inferno, meets the ferryman Charon on the banks of Acheron gathering the damned, and finally faints after an earthquake and a flash of light.
4. Entering Limbo with Virgil, Dante meets the great classical poets who welcome him into their group, then visits a noble castle where the great spirits of antiquity, including heroes and philosophers, reside in hopeless desire.
5. In the second circle, after encountering Minos, Dante sees the lustful swept by a storm and, moved by pity, listens to the story of Paolo and Francesca, ultimately fainting from compassion for them.
6. In the third circle, under a freezing rain and tormented by Cerberus, Dante meets the glutton Ciacco who prophesies Florence's misfortunes, and then learns from Virgil that the torments of the damned will increase after the Last Judgment.
7. After silencing Plutus, Dante and Virgil observe the punishment of the avaricious and the prodigal in the fourth circle, listen to the explanation of Fortune, and descend into the Stygian marsh of the fifth circle, where they see the wrathful and the sullen.
8. Phlegyas ferries Dante and Virgil across the Stygian marsh, where they meet Filippo Argenti, and they reach the walls of the city of Dis, where demons deny them entry, but Virgil announces the arrival of a heavenly messenger.
9. After overcoming the threat of the Furies and Medusa thanks to a heavenly messenger who opens the gates of Dis, the poets enter the sixth circle, where heretics are punished inside fiery sepulchers.
10. In the circle of the heretics, Dante speaks with Farinata, who prophesies his exile, and with Cavalcante, who mistakenly believes his son Guido is dead, learning about the nature of the damned's foresight.
11. While they pause to get used to the stench of the next circle, Virgil explains to Dante the moral structure of lower Hell, distinguishing the sins of malice (violence and fraud) from those of incontinence punished in the upper circles.
12. Having passed the Minotaur, Dante and Virgil descend into the first ring of the seventh circle where, guided by the centaur Nessus, they walk along the Phlegethon, observing the violent against others immersed in boiling blood before crossing the river.
13. In the wood of the suicides, where souls are transformed into trees tormented by the Harpies, Dante meets Pier della Vigna and then witnesses the infernal hunt of the wastrels pursued by black hounds.
14. Dante and Virgil arrive in the round of the violent against God, a land of fiery sand where they meet the proud blasphemer Capaneus and Virgil explains the origin of the infernal rivers from the statue of the Old Man of Crete.
15. Walking on a bank across the burning sand, Dante meets his master Brunetto Latini, who prophesies his exile; Dante expresses his gratitude before Brunetto commends his "Tesoro" to him and runs away.
16. Dante meets three noble Florentines, discusses the city's decline with them, and then watches as Virgil throws a cord into the abyss to summon a marvelous figure from the depths.
17. Geryon, the personification of Fraud, appears, and after Dante speaks with the usurers identified by their coats of arms, the two poets mount the monster's back and descend into the abyss of the eighth circle.
18. In the Eighth Circle, Malebolge, Dante and Virgil observe the punishment of the panders and seducers, whipped by demons in the first ditch, and that of the flatterers, immersed in human excrement in the second.
19. In the third bolgia, Dante meets Pope Nicholas III among the simonists, hears his prophecy on the damnation of Boniface VIII and Clement V, and delivers a harsh invective against papal corruption, earning Virgil's approval.
20. Dante observes the diviners forced to walk backward and weeps, but Virgil rebukes him and points out several figures, dwelling at length on the story of the prophetess Manto to explain the origins of Mantua.
21. In the fifth ditch, Dante and Virgil encounter the Malebranche demons who torment the barrators in boiling pitch; their leader Malacoda, after speaking with Virgil, assigns them an escort of ten devils to guide them to a bridge he falsely claims is intact.
22. A Navarrese barrator, captured by the Malebranche, cleverly tricks them to dive back into the pitch, sparking a brawl between two demons who also fall into the pond, thus allowing Dante and Virgil to depart.
23. Having fled the Malebranche, Dante and Virgil reach the ditch of the hypocrites, where they meet Catalano and Caiaphas and learn from a friar about a demon's deception regarding a collapsed bridge, which angers Virgil.
24. After a strenuous climb, Dante reaches the bolgia of thieves, a pit swarming with snakes, where he witnesses the metamorphosis of the damned Vanni Fucci, who confesses his sacrilegious theft and delivers an angry political prophecy.
25. After the punishment of Vanni Fucci and the appearance of the centaur Cacus, Dante witnesses the horrific and complex metamorphoses of the Florentine thieves, who either fuse with serpents or exchange their forms with them.
26. Having arrived in the eighth bolgia, where fraudulent counselors are wrapped in flames, Dante listens to Ulysses recount his final voyage beyond the Pillars of Hercules, undertaken to 'follow virtue and knowledge' and ending in a tragic shipwreck.
27. Dante meets Guido da Montefeltro, who, after asking for news of Romagna, recounts how he was damned for fraudulent counsel given to Boniface VIII, who offered him a preemptive absolution that proved invalid against diabolical logic.
28. In the ninth ditch, Dante observes the punishment of the sowers of discord, mutilated by a devil, and meets figures like Muhammad, Pier da Medicina, and Mosca de' Lamberti, culminating in the shocking vision of Bertran de Born carrying his severed head for having divided father and son.
29. After discussing his kinsman Geri del Bello, Dante reaches the final pouch where falsifiers are punished with scabs and speaks with two alchemists, Griffolino d'Arezzo and Capocchio, who comments ironically on the vanity of the Sienese.
30. Dante meets the rabid Gianni Schicchi and the coiner Master Adam in the pit of the Falsifiers, witnesses the latter's vulgar quarrel with Sinon, and is finally rebuked by Virgil for taking interest in such a base spectacle.
31. Arriving at the central pit dividing the eighth and ninth circles, Dante and Virgil encounter the Giants, including Nimrod and Ephialtes, and are lowered to the frozen bottom of Cocytus by the giant Antaeus, persuaded by Virgil's words.
32. Having reached the frozen lake of Cocytus, Dante encounters traitors to their country and kin, including the Alberti brothers and Bocca degli Abati, before discovering Count Ugolino devouring the head of Archbishop Ruggieri.
33. Count Ugolino recounts the harrowing story of his death by starvation along with his sons before resuming gnawing on the skull of the traitor Ruggieri; the poets then proceed into Tolomea, where Friar Alberigo explains that the souls of traitors to guests arrive in Hell before their bodies die.
34. Dante and Virgil, after seeing Lucifer chewing the three arch-traitors, climb down his body, pass the center of the Earth, and ascend a hidden path to finally exit Hell and see the stars again.
