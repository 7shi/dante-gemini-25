1. Arriving with Virgil on the shore of Purgatory, Dante meets Cato who, upon learning of their divine mission, orders Virgil to purify his disciple by washing his face and girding him with a miraculously regrowing reed.
2. After an angelic boatman arrives on the shore of Purgatory with a host of souls, Dante meets his friend Casella who sings a sweet song, but they are all harshly rebuked by Cato, who urges them to begin their purification.
3. After Virgil's explanation of his nature as a spirit and the limits of human reason, the poets meet the excommunicated at the foot of the steep mountain, among whom Manfred recounts his salvation and asks his daughter Constance for prayers to shorten his wait.
4. After the arduous ascent to the first terrace of Ante-Purgatory, Dante receives an astronomy lesson from Virgil and then meets his friend Belacqua among the negligent, who explains the law of their waiting.
5. In Purgatorio Canto 5, Dante meets the souls of the violently slain who repented at the last moment, who crowd around him asking for prayers, and he listens to the stories of Iacopo del Cassero, Bonconte da Montefeltro, and Pia de' Tolomei.
6. After discussing the efficacy of prayer with Virgil, Dante meets Sordello, whose embrace with his fellow countryman triggers the poet's vehement invective against the political division of Italy and the instability of Florence.
7. Sordello, after welcoming Virgil and explaining that they cannot climb at night, leads the poets to a flowery valley where he points out the souls of negligent rulers awaiting the dawn.
8. In the Valley of the Rulers, Dante witnesses the arrival of two angels who drive away a serpent, then speaks with his friend Nino Visconti and with Currado Malaspina, who prophesies his future exile.
9. After a prophetic dream, Dante awakens at the entrance to Purgatory where, having been carried by Lucia, he is admitted by the guardian angel who inscribes seven P's on his forehead before opening the gate with two keys.
10. Having climbed to the first terrace of Purgatory, Dante and Virgil admire divinely sculpted bas-reliefs depicting examples of humility before encountering the proud, who atone for their sin crushed beneath heavy stones.
11. On the first terrace, Dante meets the proud who, crushed by weights, are praying; here Omberto Aldobrandeschi, Oderisi da Gubbio, and Provenzan Salvani exemplify the vanity of earthly glory and the value of a single act of humility.
12. Dante observes the examples of punished pride on the pavement, then an angel erases the 'P' from his forehead and guides him toward the upper terrace, making the ascent lighter.
13. In the circle of the envious, Dante meets the souls with their eyes sewn shut with iron wire and speaks with Sapìa of Siena, who recounts her joy at the defeat of her fellow citizens and asks for prayers to hasten her purification.
14. Having met Guido del Duca and Rinieri da Calboli, Dante hears Guido's violent invective against the Arno valley and his lament for the fallen Romagna, before hearing the voices of punished envy and Virgil's rebuke to humanity.
15. Invited by an angel to ascend and instructed by Virgil on divine love, Dante has a vision of examples of meekness on the third terrace, where he is then enveloped by a dense, blinding smoke.
16. In the smoke of the third terrace, Dante meets Marco Lombardo, who, after discussing free will, attributes the world's corruption to the Church's confusion of spiritual and temporal powers.
17. After witnessing visions of punished wrath, Dante ascends to the fourth terrace where, halted by night, he listens to Virgil explain the theory of love as the seed of every virtue and sin and the resulting structure of Purgatory.
18. Virgil explains the nature of love and free will to Dante, then they encounter the souls of the slothful, who run while shouting examples of zeal and punished sloth, until Dante, overwhelmed by his thoughts, falls asleep.
19. Woken by a dream about a deceitful siren and spurred on by Virgil and an angel, Dante ascends to the fifth terrace, where Pope Adrian V explains the punishment of the avaricious and asks him for prayers.
20. Meeting Hugh Capet, who prophesies the wicked deeds of the French kings, Dante witnesses a mysterious earthquake shake Purgatory, followed by the souls' choral hymn 'Gloria in excelsis Deo'.
21. While Dante and Virgil wonder about an earthquake, they meet the soul of the poet Statius, who explains that the tremor marks his completed purification and, after declaring his admiration for the Aeneid, discovers with astonishment that his guide is Virgil himself.
22. After ascending to the sixth terrace, Statius reveals to Virgil that he was prodigal and converted to Christianity thanks to Virgil's works, before the three poets encounter a tree from which a voice lists examples of temperance.
23. On the terrace of the gluttonous, Dante meets his friend Forese Donati, who explains the purifying punishment and the efficacy of his wife Nella's prayers, before hearing Dante's account of his journey guided by Virgil.
24. Continuing his conversation with Forese, who prophesies the terrible end of his brother Corso, Dante defines the 'sweet new style' to Bonagiunta of Lucca before passing the second tree and having the angel erase the sixth 'P'.
25. While Statius explains to Dante the generation of the soul and the formation of aerial bodies, the poets reach the seventh terrace where the lustful purify themselves in fire, singing examples of chastity.
26. In the circle of the lustful, who are divided into two groups, Dante meets the poet Guido Guinizzelli, who after a moving dialogue points out the 'better craftsman' Arnaut Daniel, who introduces himself in Provençal before hiding in the fire that refines them.
27. Spurred by the name of Beatrice, Dante crosses the wall of fire, dreams of Leah as a symbol of the active life, and upon reaching the top of the stairway, hears Virgil's final words crowning him lord of himself.
28. Having entered the divine forest, the narrator meets a solitary woman, Matelda, who from beyond a river explains the divine origin of the place's phenomena and reveals that this is the Eden the ancient poets sang of as the Golden Age.
29. Following Matelda, Dante witnesses the sudden appearance of a mystical procession led by seven candelabra, which includes twenty-four elders, four living creatures, a triumphal chariot pulled by a griffin flanked by the virtues, and other biblical figures, which halts before him.
30. In the Earthly Paradise, Beatrice appears and, after Virgil's disappearance, harshly rebukes Dante for going astray after her death, forcing him to a tearful repentance as a condition for salvation.
31. Dante, prompted to confession by Beatrice, is immersed in the Lethe by Matelda to be purified, then led by the virtues he gazes into Beatrice's eyes, where he contemplates the Griffin before she unveils her smile.
32. After the Chariot is tied to the tree and Dante awakens from a mystical sleep, Beatrice commands him to observe the series of allegorical attacks that transform it into a monster, finally dragged away by a prostitute and a giant.
33. Beatrice entrusts Dante with the obscure DXV prophecy and, after clarifying the nature of his forgetfulness, has Matelda immerse him in the Eunoè, completing his purification and making him ready to ascend to the stars.
