1. After the invocation to Apollo, Dante begins his ascent to Paradise with Beatrice, experiencing 'transhumanization' and, overwhelmed by doubts about his bodily ascent, receives from her the explanation of the universal order that naturally brings him back to God.
2. After the proem to the readers, Dante ascends with Beatrice to the Moon and, questioning her about the spots, listens to her refutation of human error and the theological explanation based on the differentiated influence of angelic intelligences.
3. In the Heaven of the Moon, Dante meets the souls of those who did not keep their vows, speaks with Piccarda Donati who explains how their will conforms to God's, and learns her story and that of Empress Constance.
4. Beatrice resolves Dante's doubts about the Platonic doctrine of souls and about divine justice towards those who suffer violence, distinguishing between absolute and relative will and prompting a new question about vows.
5. Beatrice explains the doctrine of vows, then ascends with Dante into the heaven of Mercury where, among a thousand joyful souls, the poet questions a spirit who shines with more intense light before replying.
6. The Emperor Justinian recounts the history of the imperial eagle to condemn the Guelphs and Ghibellines, explains that in the Heaven of Mercury are the spirits who acted for fame, and points out the soul of Romeo di Villanova.
7. After Justinian's departure, Beatrice, intuiting Dante's doubts, explains the justice of the Crucifixion, the necessity of the Incarnation for human redemption, and the doctrine of creation and the resurrection of bodies.
8. In the Heaven of Venus, Dante meets Charles Martel, who, after lamenting his short life and criticizing his brother Robert, explains how Divine Providence assigns different inclinations to individuals for the good of society, regardless of lineage.
9. In the Heaven of Venus, Dante speaks with the souls of Cunizza da Romano and Folco of Marseilles, who deliver harsh prophecies against contemporary corruption and explain how their past amorous inclination has been transformed into blessedness.
10. Having ascended to the Heaven of the Sun, Dante meets the first crown of twelve wise spirits who, dancing and singing, form a luminous garland, and one of them, Thomas Aquinas, introduces himself and names the other eleven blessed souls.
11. Thomas Aquinas resolves Dante's doubts by narrating the life of Saint Francis, guide of the Church and husband of Lady Poverty, and then laments the corruption of his own Dominican order.
12. A second crown of wise spirits appears, and from it Bonaventure of Bagnoregio praises the life of St. Dominic, laments the corruption of his own Franciscan order, and finally introduces himself and the other eleven blessed souls.
13. Thomas Aquinas resolves Dante's doubt about Solomon's wisdom, explaining that his 'kingly prudence' is distinct from the unique perfection of Adam and Christ and warning against hasty judgments.
14. After Solomon explains how the souls' light will increase with their resurrected bodies, Dante ascends with Beatrice to the Heaven of Mars, where innumerable warrior spirits form a splendid cross upon which Christ flashes.
15. In the Heaven of Mars, Dante meets the soul of his great-great-grandfather Cacciaguida, who affectionately welcomes him, recalls ancient and virtuous Florence in contrast to its modern state, and recounts his life up to his martyrdom in the Holy Land.
16. At Dante's request, Cacciaguida describes the small and virtuous Florence of his time, listing the ancient noble families and identifying the origin of its ruin in the mixing with new people and the feud sparked by the Buondelmonti.
17. Cacciaguida predicts Dante's painful exile from Florence and future refuge in Verona, and facing the poet's doubts, urges him to faithfully report his entire vision, as his testimony, though harsh, will be vital nourishment for posterity.
18. After seeing the warrior spirits in Mars, Dante ascends to Jupiter where the holy souls, flying like birds, compose the writing on divine justice and then form the head of an eagle, a symbol that prompts the poet to inveigh against the Church's corruption.
19. The Eagle, formed by the souls of the just, responds to Dante's doubt about the salvation of virtuous pagans by explaining the inscrutability of divine justice and prophesying the condemnation of many wicked Christian kings.
20. The Eagle identifies the six just rulers forming its eye and, responding to Dante's doubt, explains the mystery of the salvation of the pagans Trajan and Ripheus through inscrutable divine grace and predestination.
21. Arriving in the heaven of Saturn, Dante sees a golden ladder on which contemplative spirits descend; one of them, Peter Damian, speaks to him of the mystery of predestination and Church corruption, before all the souls emit a deafening cry.
22. In the heaven of Saturn, Saint Benedict denounces the corruption of his order before ascending the celestial ladder, after which Dante ascends with Beatrice to the Fixed Stars and, from his constellation, observes the Earth's smallness.
23. After the blinding vision of Christ's triumph, Dante contemplates the garden of the blessed where he witnesses the coronation of the Virgin Mary by Gabriel, her ascension, and finally the triumph of Saint Peter and the other saints.
24. At Beatrice's request, Saint Peter examines Dante on Faith, and the poet, by defining its nature and origin and professing its substance, passes the test, earning the apostle's joyful approval.
25. Examined by St. James on Hope, Dante answers correctly, but when St. John the Evangelist arrives, the poet stares at his splendor to see his body and is blinded by it.
26. After passing the examination on Charity with St. John, Dante regains his sight and meets Adam, who, reading his mind, answers his questions on original sin, his time on Earth and in Limbo, the primordial language, and his stay in Eden.
27. After Saint Peter's violent invective against papal corruption, Dante ascends to the Primum Mobile where Beatrice explains its nature and prophesies humanity's future redemption.
28. Dante contemplates the divine point surrounded by the nine angelic orders, whose speed is inverse to that of the material heavens, and Beatrice explains the reason for this and the correct hierarchy established by Dionysius.
29. Beatrice clarifies Dante's doubts about the creation of the angels, their fall, and their innumerable nature, harshly condemning preachers on earth who distort the Gospel for vainglory.
30. Having arrived in the Empyrean, Dante sees a river of light transform into the celestial rose of the blessed, where Beatrice shows him the throne destined for Henry VII and prophesies the damnation of Pope Clement V.
31. Having arrived in the Empyrean, Dante admires the white rose of the blessed, takes leave of Beatrice who returns to her throne, and welcomes St. Bernard as his new guide, who invites him to contemplate the Virgin Mary.
32. St. Bernard illustrates to Dante the arrangement of the blessed in the Celestial Rose, divided between believers in Christ to come and Christ who has come and the souls of children, and, after pointing out the main figures, invites him to look at Mary to prepare for the final vision.
33. Guided by St. Bernard's prayer to the Virgin, Dante attains the supreme vision of the Trinity and the mystery of the Incarnation, until a divine flash pacifies his mind and his will is aligned with the Love that moves the universe.
