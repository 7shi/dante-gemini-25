1. Dante invokes Apollo to sing of Paradise, contemplates the sun with Beatrice and, transhumanized, naturally rises beyond earth as Beatrice explains the providential order guiding every creature to its own end.
2. Dante and Beatrice ascend to the Moon, where Beatrice refutes the explanation of its spots based on differing density and attributes them to the different formal virtue infused into the heavens by divine order.
3. In the Heaven of the Moon, guided by Beatrice, Dante recognizes in the souls of Piccarda and Constance, unwillingly failing in their vows, the perfect peace of those who fully conform their will to God's.
4. Beatrice resolves Dante’s doubts about the abode of blessed souls and the responsibility of those who yield to violence in vows, distinguishing absolute from relative will; heartened, Dante asks whether broken vows can be compensated.
5. Beatrice explains to Dante the inviolability of the vow and warns against rash promises, then leads him into the heaven of Mercury, where shining souls welcome him and one of them prepares to reveal itself.
6. Justinian, in the heaven of Mercury, recounts the providential history of the Roman eagle, condemns Guelphs and Ghibellines who abuse it, explains the condition of spirits active for fame, and points out the just Romeo, unjustly slandered.
7. As the blessed lights depart, Beatrice resolves Dante’s doubts about the double justice of the cross and redemption, explaining the Incarnation, the nature of creatures, and the direct origin of the human soul, from which resurrection follows.
8. In the heaven of Venus Dante meets Charles Martel, who recalls the consequences of his early death and explains how providence ordains diverse natures and inclinations, while the world errs by contradicting them.
9. In the Heaven of Venus, Cunizza prophesies the sins and punishments of the Marca, while Folco celebrates Rahab and condemns Florence and the Church’s corruption caused by the florin.
10. Dante ascends with Beatrice into the Sun, where a crown of wise spirits sings and dances around them, and Thomas Aquinas introduces himself and the other blessed souls, celebrating their heavenly harmony.
11. Thomas Aquinas, reading Dante’s doubts, celebrates Saint Francis, model of poverty and approved founder of his order, as one of the two guides Providence gave the Church with Saint Dominic, and deplores the Dominicans’ corruption.
12. As a second crown of wise spirits joins the first in harmonious song, Saint Bonaventure celebrates Saint Dominic, recounts his mission against heresy, denounces the Franciscans’ decline, and presents the blessed spirits surrounding him.
13. While two crowns of wise spirits dance and sing around Dante and Beatrice, Thomas Aquinas clarifies that Solomon's unequaled wisdom was kingly prudence, distinguishes Adam's and the Virgin's perfection, and warns against hasty judgments.
14. Beatrice has the blessed answer Dante’s question about light after the resurrection, then Dante ascends with her to Mars, where he contemplates Christ in a luminous cross and hears a song of praise.
15. In the heaven of Mars, Cacciaguida, Dante’s ancestor, reveals his affection and identity, recalls ancient sober Florence, and recounts his knightly life and martyrdom in the Holy Land.
16. Dante asks Cacciaguida about ancient Florence and their lineage, and his ancestor contrasts the small, pure, peaceful city of his time with Florence corrupted by newcomers and by the conflicts begun with Buondelmonte.
17. Dante asks Cacciaguida to clarify the predictions about his future, and he announces his exile, the support of the Scaligeri, and Cangrande's glory, then commands him to recount the entire vision of the afterlife without fear.
18. Cacciaguida shows Dante the warrior spirits of the cross; then, having ascended with Beatrice to Jupiter’s heaven, he sees just souls form the inscription on justice and become the luminous eagle, before which he condemns the Church’s corruption.
19. The luminous eagle of just souls resolves Dante’s doubt about divine justice, affirming its inscrutability and condemning corrupt Christian rulers who, though invoking Christ, will be judged more harshly than virtuous non-Christians.
20. The Eagle indicates the six righteous souls that form its eye, including Trajan and Ripheus, and explains that their salvation, obtained through faith and divine grace, reveals the inscrutability of predestination.
21. In the Heaven of Saturn, Beatrice prepares Dante for the vision of the golden ladder and the contemplative souls, including Peter Damian, who explains the celestial silence, denounces corrupt prelates, and prompts an incomprehensible cry.
22. In Saturn’s heaven Saint Benedict denounces the corruption of monks and foretells divine aid; then Dante rises with Beatrice to the stars of Gemini, from which he contemplates the spheres he has crossed and the small Earth.
23. In the heaven of the Fixed Stars, Beatrice shows Dante Christ’s triumph and Mary crowned by Gabriel, while the blessed souls sing the Queen of Heaven and the poet contemplates their ineffable glory.
24. Beatrice entrusts Dante to Saint Peter for an examination on faith; the poet defines its nature, foundation, and content, professing one and triune God, and the apostle, satisfied, approves, blesses, and dances around him.
25. Dante hopes to return to Florence crowned as a poet, then Saint James questions him about hope, which he professes as expectation of glory, while Saint John reveals his error about his body and leaves him blinded, unable to see Beatrice.
26. Healed of blindness by Beatrice, Dante professes his love for God to Saint John, then meets Adam, who explains original sin, his waiting and earthly life, and the changeability of human languages.
27. As Heaven sings and Saint Peter condemns the Church’s corruption, ordering Dante to denounce it, the souls ascend and Beatrice leads him into the Primum Mobile, where she explains celestial order and prophesies the world’s imminent renewal.
28. Dante contemplates the divine point around which the nine angelic choirs revolve, and Beatrice explains their order, their relation to the material heavens, and the hierarchy revealed by Dionysius.
29. Beatrice explains to Dante the creation and hierarchy of the angels, Lucifer’s rebellion and the faithful angels’ differing grace, condemns the errors of philosophers and preachers, then celebrates the innumerable variety of angelic light and love.
30. Dante, dazzled and purified in the Empyrean, contemplates with Beatrice the celestial rose and the two courts of Heaven, while she shows him Henry’s seat and prophesies papal corruption.
31. Dante contemplates the white rose of the blessed and the flight of the angels, thanks Beatrice, who appears in her seat, and, guided by Saint Bernard, turns his gaze to Mary, surrounded by rejoicing angels.
32. Saint Bernard explains to Dante the order of the celestial rose, clarifying the fate of children and the divine distribution of grace, then guides him to contemplate Mary and invokes from her the aid needed for him to rise to Christ.
33. Saint Bernard prays to the Virgin to grant Dante the supreme vision; answered, Dante contemplates in the divine light the unity of creation and the Trinitarian mystery with Christ's human image, until his will accords with the love that moves the sun and stars.
