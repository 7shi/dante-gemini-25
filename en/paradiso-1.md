1. After invoking Apollo, Dante gazes upon Beatrice, begins his supernatural ascent to Heaven, and learns from her that his journey is a natural return to the divine order.
2. After ascending with Beatrice into the Moon, Dante's incorrect theory about its dark spots is refuted and replaced by her complex metaphysical explanation involving the differentiated influence of divine virtue.
3. In the Heaven of the Moon, Dante meets Piccarda Donati, who explains that the souls who broke their vows are perfectly content because their peace resides in aligning their will with God's.
4. Beatrice answers Dante's unspoken doubts, refuting Plato's theory of souls and explaining how a compromised will diminishes merit, which prompts Dante to pose a new question about compensating for broken vows.
5. Beatrice explains the sacred nature of vows before ascending with Dante to Mercury, where he is welcomed by joyful souls and asks one to reveal its identity.
6. The soul of Emperor Justinian recounts the divine history of the Roman eagle, condemns its political misuse, and explains that the spirits in Mercury are those who sought earthly honor.
7. Beatrice answers Dante's unspoken questions by explaining the divine justice behind the Crucifixion and the necessity of the Incarnation for humanity's redemption and eventual resurrection.
8. In the sphere of Venus, the narrator met Charles Martel, who explained how divine providence shapes individual natures and why society suffers when people are forced into unsuitable roles.
9. In the Heaven of Venus, the souls of Cunizza and Folco joyfully recount their amorous pasts before delivering harsh prophecies against the political and ecclesiastical corruption of Italy and the Church.
10. Ascending to the Sun, Dante is encircled by a crown of twelve wise souls, from which Thomas Aquinas introduces himself and names each of his brilliant companions.
11. The Dominican Thomas Aquinas recounts the life of St. Francis of Assisi to Dante to clarify a previous statement, praising him as a champion of the Church before lamenting his own order's decline.
12. From a second circle of wise souls, St. Bonaventure praises St. Dominic's life, laments the corruption of his own Franciscan order, and introduces his eleven companions.
13. Thomas Aquinas explains that King Solomon’s unequaled wisdom was a specific kingly prudence, not absolute perfection, and warns Dante against making hasty judgments about philosophy, faith, or salvation.
14. After Solomon explains the eternal radiance of resurrected bodies, Dante ascends to Mars and beholds warrior souls forming a brilliant, singing cross of light upon which Christ's image flashes.
15. In the Heaven of Mars, Dante meets his crusader ancestor Cacciaguida, who praises the virtues of old Florence and recounts his life and martyrdom in the service of Emperor Conrad III.
16. At Dante's request, his ancestor Cacciaguida nostalgically lists the great families of 12th-century Florence, contrasting its past purity with its present corruption and identifying the Buondelmonti affair as the source of its ruin.
17. Dante's ancestor Cacciaguida foretells his painful exile from Florence but commands him to truthfully record his entire divine journey, assuring him it will bring lasting fame.
18. Dante sees warrior-saints in Mars before ascending to Jupiter, where souls of the just spell out a message of justice and form an imperial eagle, prompting him to condemn papal corruption.
19. The celestial Eagle addresses the narrator's doubt about divine justice for non-Christians, explaining God's inscrutable will before condemning the sins of corrupt contemporary kings.
20. The celestial Eagle identified the six souls of just rulers in its eye, explaining the mysterious divine grace that allowed the pagans Trajan and Ripheus to achieve salvation.
21. In Saturn's silent heaven, Dante meets the contemplative Peter Damian, who explains divine predestination and decries church corruption before joining other souls in a thunderous, overwhelming cry.
22. Dante meets St. Benedict, who decries his order's corruption, before ascending a celestial ladder to the Fixed Stars, from where he gazes down upon the insignificant Earth.
23. In the Heaven of the Fixed Stars, Dante witnesses the glorious Triumph of Christ and then beholds the Virgin Mary, who is crowned by the angel Gabriel before ascending with the company of saints.
24. At Beatrice's request, St. Peter examines Dante on the nature and substance of Faith, blessing the poet joyfully after he successfully professes his beliefs based on scripture and reason.
25. Dante is successfully examined on Hope by St. James, meets St. John, and is temporarily blinded while trying to see the apostle's physical body.
26. St. John examines Dante on love, Beatrice restores his sight, and Adam then answers his unasked questions about his sin, original language, and brief time in Eden.
27. After St. Peter delivers a fiery condemnation of the corrupt papacy, Dante ascends with Beatrice to the ninth heaven, the Primum Mobile, where she explains its nature and prophesies humanity's future redemption.
28. Dante sees God as a point of light surrounded by nine angelic circles, and Beatrice explains their paradoxical motion, identifies their orders, and links them to the physical heavens.
29. Beatrice described the instantaneous creation and fall of the angels, correcting earthly errors about their nature and condemning the corrupt preachers who spread false doctrines on Earth.
30. Upon arriving in the Empyrean, Dante beholds the court of heaven as a vast Celestial Rose and hears Beatrice prophesy the fate of Emperor Henry VII and Pope Clement V.
31. Dante beholds the Celestial Rose, thanks Beatrice as she returns to her throne, and follows his new guide, St. Bernard, in gazing upon the Virgin Mary at the summit.
32. St. Bernard explained the structure of the Celestial Rose and the theology of infant salvation, before directing Dante's gaze to the Virgin Mary to prepare him for the ultimate vision.
33. Guided by St. Bernard's prayer to Mary, Dante received the ultimate vision of God, beholding the Trinity and Incarnation until his will was perfectly aligned with divine Love.
